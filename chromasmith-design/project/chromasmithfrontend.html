    Chromasmith — Guide

    A single-file film-emulation and color-grading tool that runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded — every image, LUT and export is processed locally on your device.

    Getting started Effects & Export Preview & zoom Match & Refine Color Copy FAQ

    Getting started

    The app has three working tabs plus this guide:

    • Effects & Export — load a photo, apply a film look (LUT preset), grain, halation, bloom and basic adjustments, then export.
    • Match & Refine — feed it a before/after pair and it builds a .cube LUT that reproduces the look.
    • Color Copy — copy the color/tone of any reference photo onto another.

    Drop an image anywhere onto a drop-zone, or click it to browse. Supported inputs: JPEG, PNG, TIFF, and Panasonic RW2 / RAW (decoded locally — large RAWs can take 15–30 s).

    Everything runs offline. The ● OFFLINE badge in the header is a feature, not an error — it confirms your photos never leave the device.

    Effects & Export tab

    Image Input

    • Drop image — load the photo you want to grade.
    • RAW profile — for RW2 files, picks the in-camera color profile (default Camera Standard) so colors match Lightroom.
    • Apply LUT — drop a .cube file or a processed LUT chart to apply a custom look.
    • Input — set to V-Log (Lumix) for V-Log stills/frame grabs: converts V-Log/V-Gamut to Rec.709 before the film look, so any normal Rec.709 .cube then applies correctly on top. Leave on Standard for normal photos.
    • Preset — pick one of the 11 built-in film looks (Velvia, Astia, Classic Neg, Provia, Reala Ace, the Beach preset, etc.).
    • Save to library / Delete — store the current LUT in this browser, or remove a saved one.
    • LUT chart — downloads a reference chart of every color. Apply your look to it in another app, drop the result back, and Chromasmith reverse-engineers it into a clean .cube.
    • Download .cube — exports the active LUT (already formatted for the Lumix Lab app).
    Recommended way to turn any Lightroom preset or profile into a .cube: use the LUT chart. Download it, apply your preset to the chart in Lightroom, export it (sRGB, full size), then drop that export back on the Apply LUT zone. Because it captures the actual rendered look empirically across the whole color cube, it's more faithful and gap-free than reconstructing a look from slider values — and the resulting .cube is ready to Download (Lumix Lab-compatible) or save to your library. Reads patch means, so any grain/sharpening/noise reduction in the export cancels out. Tip: a LUT only carries global color & tone — not local masks, sharpening or grain.

    Quick Actions & One-tap Looks

    • Enable All FX — turns on grain, halation, bloom and borders at once.
    • One-tap Looks — live thumbnails of every preset; click one to apply it.

    Basic Adjustments (toggle)

    Exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, temperature, tint, vibrance, saturation, plus Clarity and Sharpen. WB Eyedropper sets white balance from a neutral grey/white you click. Auto-enhance sets levels and white balance automatically. This card's Reset zeroes them.

    Crop & Rotate

    • ⟲ / ⟳ 90° rotate, ⇄ / ⇅ Flip mirror, Straighten slider levels the horizon.
    • Crop opens an interactive crop overlay with aspect presets; the Transform card's Reset link clears all of it.
    Geometry applies to every loaded image (for batch exports). ⌘Z/⌘Y undo and redo it.

    Tone Curves

    • RGB / R / G / B tabs — master and per-channel point curves. Click the graph to add a point, drag to shape, double-click a point to remove it. Endpoints slide vertically (lift blacks / fade highlights).
    Curves apply after Basic Adjustments and before the film FX, so halation & grain react to the curved image — like shaping the negative.

    Color Mixer (HSL)

    • Eight color bands (Red → Magenta). Pick a band chip, then shift its Hue, Saturation and Luminance independently — e.g. tame orange skin, deepen a blue sky, mute distracting greens. Edited bands get a white ring.

    Masks

    • Up to 4 Radial (faces, subjects) or Linear (skies, foregrounds) masks. Position/size with the X/Y/Width sliders, soften with Feather, flip with Invert, and restrict to a tonal range with Lum floor/ceiling (e.g. brighten only the shadows inside the mask).
    • Each mask carries its own Exposure, Contrast, Temp and Saturation. Masks are resolution-independent — exports and the 1:1 loupe match the preview exactly.
    • Set Show on photo to Selection to see the mask's effective selection in red — the shape combined with the Lum and Color range gates. Overlay and Isolate draw the shape only. O cycles the modes.

    Skin Tone (even out an uneven tan)

    • Every other color control here shifts tones by a fixed amount, which is why a patchy tan resists them: raising warmth moves a pale chest and an already-tanned face equally, so the face over-cooks while the chest still looks pale next to it. Uniformity works the other way — it pulls the selected tones toward one target, so areas far from it move a lot and areas already close barely move. Uneven skin evens out instead of the dark parts getting darker.
    • Add a + Skin mask, then click a mid-tone patch of skin. Drag the shape on the photo so it covers the person you mean — it starts in the middle of the frame, and the color range alone can't tell skin from anything sharing its hue, such as a pet, sand or bare wood. Use + Brush or − Subtract prev for anything the ellipse can't isolate.
    • Switch Show on photo to Selection, then shift-click each different kind of skin — matte, shaded, paler, pinker — until it all reads red. One pick can't cover a body: skin sits on both sides of the red hue boundary, so a gate centered on a cheek can miss a sheeny chest almost entirely. This matters more than it looks: each pixel moves by weight × strength × distance-to-target, so a weakly-selected patch barely moves — and if it's the patch furthest from the target, the difference gets worse instead of better.
    • Keep Feather low-ish. It sets where the mask's fade starts, so a high value fades from the middle of the ellipse outward and makes position, rather than color, decide how much each area moves. A low feather gives a flat center and fades only near the edge, which is what lets the tones actually meet.
    • Target is independent of what you picked, so you can aim at a tan that appears nowhere in the photo: Match pick just evens out the tone you clicked, Swatch offers the 10-shade Monk Skin Tone scale (4–6 is the useful span for tanning a light base), and Custom takes any color. Tan Depth/Warmth then nudge the target — never the pixels — so pushing them deepens the whole selection evenly.
    • Preserve modeling only affects the Lightness slider. At 0 it collapses lightness onto the target, which evens out a hard tan line but flattens the shading that gives a body its form. At 100 it shifts the overall lightness level instead, leaving the shading intact. Start high.

    Film FX

    • Film Grain — Amount, Size, and grain character. Rendered at true export scale in the 1:1 view (see below).
    • Film Artifacts — dust & hairs, vertical scratches and a warm light leak, applied to the "negative" before the print stock. Placement is seeded (export matches preview); Reshuffle re-rolls it.
    • Halation — the red-orange glow real film gives bright edges. Amount, Radius, and Shadow protect (raise it if dark eyes surrounded by bright skin flood red).
    • Bloom — soft highlight glow. Amount, Threshold, Radius.
    • Vignette — darkens the corners.
    • Borders — two stackable coloured frames with adjustable thickness.
    • Canvas — mats the photo (with its borders) onto an aspect-ratio canvas: pick a ratio (4:5 keeps Instagram from cropping a 2:3 photo), zoom the photo on the canvas, and choose a background color or a blurred copy of the photo.
    The halation & bloom are calibrated to match Dehancer's film-emulation reference. Every effect is a toggle — off by default, so a bare LUT export stays clean.

    Export

    • Filename / Format / Quality — Auto picks PNG for lossless sources and JPEG otherwise.
    • Export renders at full original resolution (not the preview size). Batch exports show a progress bar and a Cancel button.
    • When more than one photo is loaded, an All photos / Current photo switch appears above Export — export the whole batch, or just the one you're previewing.
    • Save session / Load session — remember all slider settings in this browser between visits.
    Loading several photos shows a filmstrip of thumbnails below the preview — click one to switch which photo you're previewing/editing. The same effects and geometry (crop/rotate/straighten) apply to every loaded photo; if one photo in a batch export fails (e.g. too large for this device), the rest still export — check the log for which one was skipped.

    Preview, zoom & the 1:1 loupe

    • Scroll / pinch to zoom, drag to pan, double-click to reset. The image always stays inside the frame.
    • The bottom-left overlay has −, a % readout, +, Fit, and 1:1.
    • 1:1 renders a native-resolution crop through the full pipeline, so grain & halation appear at their true export size — the regular zoom only magnifies the preview. Use it to pixel-peep before exporting.
    • Hold to compare shows the untouched original while pressed; Split gives a draggable before/after divider; Histogram overlays live RGB levels.

    Match & Refine tab

    Builds a LUT from an example. Best when you have the same photo before and after grading (e.g. a flat export and the Lightroom result).

    • Before / Original — the ungraded flat image.
    • After / Target — the graded result you want to reproduce.
    • Starting LUT / Preset (optional) — a .cube or .xmp to bootstrap the fit.
    • Name auto-fills from the target filename.
    • Generate LUT & XMP — produces a downloadable .cube (plus an .xmp) and adds it to history with a per-color summary of what changed.
    A LUT captures global color & tone only — it can't reproduce sharpening, noise reduction, grain, or local masks. The output .cube is Lumix Lab-ready.

    Color Copy tab

    Transfers the overall color and tone of one photo onto another — no before/after pair needed.

    • Source image — the photo to grade.
    • Reference image — the photo whose look you want.
    • Download .cube LUT / Download .xmp Preset — export the matched grade, with a summary of the characteristics copied.

    This is a per-channel histogram match — great for a quick mood transfer, looser than a true Match & Refine fit.

    FAQ

    Are my photos uploaded anywhere?
    No. All decoding, grading and exporting runs in your browser with WebGL. The app works fully offline. The one exception is the optional Google Photos connection below — and only when you use it.
    Why does the header say OFFLINE?
    It's confirming local-only processing — that's the intended state, not a connection error.
    Can I use Google Photos?
    Yes — optional and off by default. Google Photos… (Image Input) opens Google's own picker so you can filter and select photos from your library, and Upload exports (Export panel) mirrors every export into a “Chromasmith” album. It needs a one-time free setup at console.cloud.google.com, and the steps differ slightly depending on whether you're using the browser/GitHub Pages build or the desktop app:

    1) create a project, 2) enable the Google Photos Picker API and Photos Library API (APIs & Services → Library), 3) configure the OAuth consent screen (External → stay in Testing, add your own Gmail as a test user), then:

    Browser / GitHub Pages: create Credentials → OAuth client ID → Web application and add this app's address to BOTH lists — Authorized JavaScript origins gets the origin (e.g. https://yourname.github.io) and Authorized redirect URIs gets the full page URL (e.g. https://yourname.github.io/Chroma-App/chromasmith-22.html).

    Desktop app: create Credentials → OAuth client ID → Desktop app instead — no origins or redirect URIs to register at all; sign-in opens your system's default browser and completes over a local loopback connection (127.0.0.1), then hands control back to the app.

    Either way, paste the client ID when the app asks (change it later via ID…). Notes: selection happens in Google's picker — the API no longer allows apps to browse your library directly; uploads count against your Google storage; sign-in lasts about an hour (the app re-prompts automatically); on the browser build, allow pop-ups for this site.
    Can I edit a photo in Chromasmith from inside Lightroom?
    Desktop app only. Lightroom Classic: Preferences → External Editing → Additional External Editor → choose Chromasmith (16-bit TIFF, sRGB recommended — the pipeline is sRGB/Rec.709). Then right-click a photo → Edit In → Chromasmith. Lightroom (cloud/CC): right-click a photo → Edit In → pick Chromasmith the first time (same TIFF handoff). Either way, edit as normal — a new Save to Lightroom button appears next to the regular Export button while a handoff photo is open. Use it to write straight back over the file Lightroom gave you (Lightroom re-imports and syncs the result automatically); the normal Export button is still there too, unchanged, for a regular local export of the same photo.
    Can I browse my Lightroom cloud photos?
    Desktop app only, and it needs your own Adobe API client (Adobe Developer Console → create a project → Add API → Lightroom Services → OAuth credential type Native App). Unlike Google Cloud Console's self-serve testing mode, Adobe's Lightroom Partner APIs have historically needed Adobe's own approval on top of just creating a project — it may not work immediately even with a real client ID. A Native App credential redirects through a custom URL scheme (not a plain localhost port like Google's), which is fixed per-project — if you create your own Adobe integration rather than using an existing one, the app's redirect URI constant needs updating to match yours. Once connected (Image Input → Lightroom → Connect), pick an album from the dropdown and click a thumbnail to pull that photo straight into the editor.
    My RW2 / RAW colors don't match Lightroom.
    First check what's showing your comparison — macOS Preview shows the camera's embedded JPEG (warmer/darker), not the real RAW render. Also pick the matching RAW profile. The remaining difference is usually just Lightroom's default noise reduction and sharpening, which a LUT can't carry.
    A .cube looks wrong in the Lumix Lab app.
    Lumix needs a #LUMIXPHOTOSTYLE STD tag; without it the camera assumes V-Log and the colors go off. Every Download .cube from Chromasmith already includes it.
    Why is my grain bigger in the preview than the export?
    The normal preview is downscaled, so effects look larger relative to the image. Use the 1:1 button to see grain & halation at true export scale.
    A preset looks broken on the LUT chart but fine on photos.
    Some captured LUTs have garbage in fully-saturated corners that real photos rarely hit. Always sanity-check a captured preset on the LUT chart, which spans the whole color cube.
    Will my settings persist?
    Use Save session to store slider settings in this browser. Saved LUTs live in the browser too — Safari may clear them after ~7 days of disuse, so keep a .cube copy of anything important.
    It looks like an old version.
    The build date is shown in the header. If it's stale, hard-refresh to clear the cached page.
