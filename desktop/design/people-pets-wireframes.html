Wireframes · for review

Naming the faces you already found

Chromasmith detects faces, embeds them, clusters them, and can recognise a specific animal. The recognition works. The interface around it barely exists.

People are a flat list of text rows in the Library sidebar — the backend returns a cover_face_id and exposes catalog_photo_faces, but the UI calls neither, so there is not one face thumbnail anywhere in the app. You name people from a faceless list, and merge by retyping a name from memory. Meanwhile pets live in a separate store, under a separate word ("Subjects"), inside the photo-grading panel.

These wireframes unify the two and give the whole thing the loop it's missing: see the face, confirm or reject, name it, move to the next.

The actual complaint, and the fix

Named vs unnamed is the split

An auto group called "Person 14" is not information — it's a to-do item. It belongs in a counted backlog row, not in the navigation. Naming one promotes it into the list; that's the only way rows appear.

Bounded scroll

The named list caps around 6 rows and scrolls internally, so Folders and Keywords sit at a stable position no matter how many people you have. The two buckets are pinned outside the scroll box so they're always reachable.

Row anatomy

22px circular crop · name · count. A pet carries a corner glyph rather than a separate section — species is an attribute of a subject, not a different kind of object.

Scan becomes Review

The header's unlabelled 13px search glyph is replaced by a stateful action: Scan when empty, Review 40 › when there's a backlog — which is now the only route into unnamed clusters, and exactly where you want people to go.

Sort

Named list by descending count, as today. Consider pinning a "Favourites" subset once someone has more than ~15 named people.

Review mode · a full-window pass over the backlog

Naming is a picker, not a text box

Typing filters existing people with their faces shown, so merging happens naturally at the moment of naming — and failure 8 disappears without a merge dialog being involved at all.

Deselect ≠ delete

An excluded face returns to Unnamed. Nothing is destroyed by getting a cluster slightly wrong, which is what makes the loop safe to run fast.

Ignore is first-class

X sends a cluster to Ignored — the background strangers you never want proposed again.

Prior art

Photos' "Add Name" queue, Lightroom's People stack, digiKam's confirm/reject wall. All three converge on this shape; the common thread is that naming is a session, not a per-item chore.

Split · the missing inverse of merge

Merge is many-to-one

The backend is pairwise today. The UI collecting a multi-select and the command looping is fine — but it should be one undoable transaction, not N calls.

Split needs new backend

catalog_split_person and catalog_set_face_person don't exist. They are small, and they are what turn a wrong merge from permanent into a mistake.

"Send back to Unnamed"

The safest of the four options, and deliberately listed — recovery should never require knowing where a face is going.

P

Real pet detection

12

Pets get the same automatic pipeline as faces — detect, crop, cluster, review — rather than the current teach-then-find. That's a new detector and a new embedding, and it comes with one honest caveat worth designing around.

The research says detection is easy and identity is hard. Immich, the closest comparable product, stalled on exactly this and concluded there is "no good enough and free model" for telling one dog from another. Finding a dog is a solved, permissively-licensed problem. So the design must expect pet clusters to be messier than face clusters — which means the review loop matters more for pets, not less, and pet suggestions should stay in the dashed "suggested" tier longer.

Identical review UI, tuned expectations

Detector: RT-DETR

Apache-2.0, ONNX-exportable, COCO animal classes. Chosen over YOLOv8/v11 specifically because Ultralytics is AGPL-3.0 — unusable in a public .dmg without open-sourcing the whole app or buying an enterprise licence.

Embedding: a licence decision

MegaDescriptor is the field standard for individual animal re-ID and would give the best clusters — but it's CC BY-NC 4.0. It would join EdgeSAM and face-parsing in LICENSES-MODELS.md as a third non-commercial model. DINOv2 (Apache-2.0) is the clean-licence fallback at some accuracy cost. Worth your call before implementation.

Species is a free label

The detector already returns a class, so a pet gets "Dog"/"Cat" automatically — useful as a filter and as a sanity check on a bad cluster, at no extra model cost.

PerSAM doesn't die

The existing teach-then-find becomes the manual assist for the case detection misses — an unusual animal, or one pet among several. It stops being the only way in.

Cost

This is the largest single item in the deck: two new vendored models, a second detect+embed pass over the library, and separate cluster tuning.

Why Info, not its own section

Everything else on that rail changes pixels. This doesn't — it's a fact about the photo, sitting with the other facts. It also means one fewer rail button, and the rail is already 14 items deep.

The Library Info panel (screen F) and this are the same list. Build it once.

WHERE NAMES ARE WRITTEN
.xmp
Per photo, beside the file. mwg-rs:Regions face rectangles + Iptc4xmpExt:PersonInImage — the same sidecar authority keywords already use. Readable by Lightroom and Bridge.
.chromasmith/
Once per library root, on the drive itself. People, pets, embeddings and face crops — so the desktop adopts the whole thing instantly rather than re-deriving it from 12,481 sidecars.

Adopt-on-mount banner, and the two things it reads

Why both

The sidecar is the interoperable record and survives the library being reorganised. The drive-root file is the fast path and carries the embeddings, which XMP has no place for.

Adopt, never auto-merge

The banner asks. Silently merging two machines' people lists is how you get duplicate Sofias with no way back.

Sidecars are the fallback

If the drive-root file is missing or stale, names still come back from the .xmp files — just slower.

Open questions for this review

Q1 · pet embedding licenceRESOLVED: real detector (screen P). One decision left — MegaDescriptor (best clusters, CC BY-NC, joins EdgeSAM/face-parsing as a third non-commercial model) vs DINOv2 (Apache-2.0, some accuracy cost). Recommend DINOv2 given the app already ships a public commercial .dmg.
Q2 · review mode's homeFull-window pass (screen C) or a panel inside the Library? Full-window is faster to work through; a panel keeps the grid visible for context.
Q3 · write XMP when?On every name, or batched on a "Save names to photos" action? Writing 12,481 sidecars during a scan is a lot of disk churn.
Q4 · confirmed vs suggestedScreen B splits them permanently. That means a face only counts once you say so — accurate, but the counts will read lower than they do today until you've done a review pass.
Q5 · Ignored's reachShould an ignored person be excluded from future clustering entirely, or just hidden? Excluding is cleaner but makes an accidental ignore harder to undo.
Q6 · face crop storageCache crops on disk beside the catalog, or decode on demand? On-demand is simpler; a review wall of 200 faces will feel it.
