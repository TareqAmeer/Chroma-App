# v9 review and the v10 plan

## Honest review of v9

**Does it sell the app's benefits?** No. It spends 13s on the problem and about 5s on the solution. The only benefit actually shown is one before/after wipe. Looks, grain, halation, RAW, the library and export are named in a grey caption for one second and never shown.

**Does it feel professional?** Partly. The typography and grid are tidy, but it's a sequence of slides with hard cuts. Nothing has depth or light, and the gate weave reads as camera shake, not film. The grade shifted the brand colours: the paper went grey and the red went orange-brown.

**Does it explain what the app does?** Not to someone who's never heard of it. "One app" says nothing about *what* it does. A viewer learns there's a red square and a beach photo.

**Following vs reading:** the square moves in one place while the copy appears in another, so the eye has to choose. The header (episode / chapter / timecode) adds a third thing to read.

## 50 improvements (v10)

### Structure and story
1. One continuous shot: the camera follows the square through a single 3D world. No cuts.
2. Spend about 60% of the runtime on the new workflow, about 25% on the old.
3. Show every key feature doing its job: Looks, Light, Colour, Texture/grain, Output/border, Library.
4. Each feature gets one beat (about 2.5s): the square lands on its tool, the photo visibly changes, a two-word label appears.
5. The old way is shown *spatially*: four separate apps far apart, with long, tiring jumps between them. Distance = friction.
6. The new way is shown spatially too: everything sits on one surface, so the hops are short, quick and happy.
7. Same photo throughout (the beach), so changes are easy to compare.
8. Open on the logo and close on it (loopable), now with the CHRO-MA-SMITH wordmark.
9. End on the full lockup with the platforms and "Free", held long enough to read.
10. Total length about 40s; trim later.

### Reading vs following
11. Captions are anchored next to the square (projected from 3D), so the eye never has to choose.
12. At most one line of copy on screen at a time.
13. Copy appears only when the square is at rest and leaves before it moves.
14. Drop the running header and timecode.
15. Copy is larger (60–72px) with a soft shadow plate, readable on a phone.

### Depth and light
16. Real 3D (three.js): the square is a bevelled red block with clearcoat.
17. Apps and the photo are physical slabs standing on a seamless studio floor, with thickness.
18. Soft real shadows; the square's shadow shrinks as it leaves the floor.
19. Environment reflections on the glossy surfaces.
20. One warm key light, a cool rim, and a soft fill.
21. Depth of field with focus pulled onto the square.
22. Atmospheric haze: far objects fade into the backdrop.
23. Foreground objects that pass close to the lens, out of focus, for parallax.
24. Lighting tells the story: it goes colder and dimmer in the old way, near-dark at the low point, warm when the app arrives.
25. A light sweep across the app slab when it rises.

### Camera
26. A smooth, critically damped follow with no shake (gate weave removed).
27. A slight lead: the camera looks a little ahead of where the square is going.
28. Lens changes only for meaning: wide for the long old-way jumps, closer for the feature beats.
29. Slow orbit drift in long holds so nothing is ever static.
30. One dramatic crane-up reveal when the app rises.

### Animation
31. Squash and stretch along the direction of travel.
32. Anticipation before every jump.
33. Landing dust: small particles puff out on each landing.
34. Secondary motion: the slabs it lands on dip and spring back.
35. Old way: the square gets heavier (slower jumps, smaller arcs, greying).
36. New way: a quick, springy rhythm.
37. Looks: the photo cross-dissolves through real grades as the square taps.
38. Grain: push in close to the photo while real grain fades up.
39. Library: real gallery cards fly in and settle into a grid around the app.
40. Output: a white border draws around the photo, then it lifts off as a finished print.

### Colour and finish
41. Brand colours held exactly: red #ff3b1f, ink #111, paper #f2efe8. The grade is no longer applied to UI colours.
42. Film grain only (fine, subtle). No weave, no flicker, no vignette.
43. Light halation only on true highlights.
44. Real motion blur from subframes.
45. Rendered at 1920×1080 with 24fps cadence.

### Sound
46. Keep the v9 score's character (felt piano, dull old way, warm arrival). You liked it.
47. Retime it to the new beats: one tap per feature landing.
48. Make the old-way jumps sound heavier, with a lower thud each time.
49. A rising swell under the app reveal.
50. Mix to −14 LUFS.
